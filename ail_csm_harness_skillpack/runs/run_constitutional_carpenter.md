Issue: warrantless acquisition of prolonged historical CSLI

## Central correction

The original assignment incorrectly assumed a circuit split over the core Carpenter holding. The revised brief does not invent conflicting appellate decisions.

- Carpenter v. United States, 585 U.S. 296 (2018), holds that acquisition of prolonged historical CSLI is a Fourth Amendment search that generally requires a warrant supported by probable cause.
- The post-Carpenter appellate decisions do not establish a current split over that core rule; they principally concern the separate question of the good-faith exception for pre-Carpenter acquisitions.
- A valid circuit split must identify the precise legal proposition on which appellate courts actually disagree.

## Cases distinguished

- United States v. Carpenter, 819 F.3d 880 (6th Cir. 2016) — reversed by the Supreme Court; not current authority.
- United States v. Carpenter, 926 F.3d 313 (6th Cir. 2019) — good-faith exception for pre-Carpenter acquisition.
- United States v. Korte, 918 F.3d 750 (9th Cir. 2019) — good-faith exception for pre-Carpenter acquisition.
- United States v. Beverly, 943 F.3d 225 (5th Cir. 2019) — good-faith exception for pre-Carpenter acquisition.

## Conclusion

If the record establishes that the Government acquired prolonged historical CSLI without a warrant and without a recognized exception, the denial of suppression should be reversed, subject to a separate analysis of the exclusionary remedy and any independently established basis for admission.
